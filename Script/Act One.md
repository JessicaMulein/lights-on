# I Left the Lights On
## Act One: The Coat

*Book, draft 1. A line like* `{{song: 01 - Trainwreck}}` *marks where a number goes; the lyric lives in the Songs folder, and build_libretto.py stitches the two together.*

---

### Prologue

*A house, open like a dollhouse: rooms on two levels, every window dark. Center, a kitchen table with more chairs than it needs. Downstage, an upright piano with a laptop on it and a mug of tea gone cold. On the stage floor, painted white on black like a road marking: a long box and two words, KEEP CLEAR. By the front door, an empty coat hook.*

*Somewhere in the house, a music box plays.*

*JENNIFER, now, comes in and sits at the piano. For a moment she looks at the two chairs nearest the door. Then she picks out the music-box tune on the keys.*

{{song: P - I Left the Lights On}}

---

### Scene 1: The Gawkers

*A train whistle, far off. The light changes: headlights, a crossing signal. The COMPANY drifts on as GAWKERS, slowing down the way you slow down for a wreck: commuters, a nurse on her break, a teenager filming on a phone. At the edge of it all stands a man in a short-sleeved dress shirt with a clipboard. This is THE ADJUSTER. He writes things down. He will be at the edge of a lot of scenes.*

JENNIFER: *(to the audience)* Oh, don't be shy. You paid for the good seats.

{{song: 01 - Trainwreck}}

---

### Scene 2: Linen Napkins

*The Whitcomb dining room, years ago: white tablecloth, silver, linen napkins in rings. DR. WHITCOMB, a pediatrician, eats with precision. MRS. WHITCOMB sits very straight. Between them sits YOUNG JEN, about nine, in a navy blazer one size too big.*

JENNIFER: *(from the piano)* Seventeen and scared, she gave me away. That's everything I know about the first part. The second part had a dress code.

YOUNG JEN: Dad. Why did the—

DR. WHITCOMB: Elbows.

YOUNG JEN: *(elbows off)* Why did the pediatrician bring a ladder to work?

*(Nothing.)*

YOUNG JEN: Because all his patients were having growth spurts.

*(Nothing. MRS. WHITCOMB touches her napkin to her lips.)*

MRS. WHITCOMB: That's enough, sweetheart.

{{song: 02 - The Mirror}}

---

### Scene 3: The One Who Always Came

*Two short honks of a car horn outside. YOUNG JEN is at the front door before the second one. In comes LOUISE, Dr. Whitcomb's mother, in a cardigan and sensible shoes, with her car keys in one hand and a foil-covered pie tin in the other.*

LOUISE: That traffic nearly killed me. Who's hungry?

YOUNG JEN: Grandma. Why did the pediatrician bring a ladder to work?

LOUISE: Oh, for Pete's sake. Why?

YOUNG JEN: Because all his patients were having growth spurts.

*(LOUISE laughs: a big, helpless, Midwestern laugh, the first laugh anybody has had in this house. YOUNG JEN stares at her like she's never heard the sound before.)*

LOUISE: Tell me another one.

{{song: 03 - Louise}}

*(LOUISE gathers her keys. The car door; two short honks, going away. YOUNG JEN waves at the window until the car is gone.)*

JENNIFER: *(to us)* She was the one who always came.

---

### Scene 4: Danny's Party

*A basement, a few years later. Music too loud, and a strobe light somebody's dad bought. TEEN JEN, thirteen, stands against the wall in a too-big flannel. YOUNG NORA, thirteen, crosses the room and leans on the wall beside her.*

YOUNG NORA: You're not dancing either.

TEEN JEN: I'm holding up the wall.

YOUNG NORA: Want help?

*(They hold up the wall together.)*

{{song: 04 - Thirteen}}

*(Somebody calls NORA's name. She goes. At the stairs she looks back once. TEEN JEN doesn't wave.)*

JENNIFER: *(to us)* Hold on to her. She comes back.

---

### Scene 5: Keep Clear

*A high school hallway between classes: lockers slamming, a bell about to ring. TEEN JEN, fourteen, at a locker. Down the hall, CASEY, Teen Jen's best friend, cups her hands around her mouth.*

CASEY: Jennifer!

*(TEEN JEN turns on a dime. Then she freezes. Half the hallway is looking at her. She's shocked, and embarrassed, and trying very hard not to grin.)*

TEEN JEN: *(hissing)* Are you crazy?

CASEY: You turned around.

JENNIFER: *(from the piano, to us)* First time I ever heard it out loud. I turned on a dime.

*(RUTH's kitchen: warm light, a table with a wobble, a cookie jar shaped like a cow. This is the first room in the show where nobody is performing. RUTH, Casey's mother and a minister, is at the stove. CASEY and TEEN JEN come in and drop their backpacks.)*

RUTH: You're staying for dinner.

CASEY: She always stays for dinner.

RUTH: Then she's always staying for dinner.

JENNIFER: *(to us)* I went to Ruth's house a lot. She believed in me. It was the one house where I didn't have to be anybody.

*(Another day. The KEEP CLEAR box on the floor lights up. Outside the high school, afternoon. Two lanes of stopped traffic, suggested by headlights. TEEN JEN, backpack on one shoulder, looks at the stopped cars and steps into the gap.)*

{{song: 05 - Keep Clear}}

*(THE EMT and her partner tape TEEN JEN to a backboard. The EMT never looks at her face. The headlights go out one by one. The box stays lit.)*

---

### Scene 6: Exile

JENNIFER: *(from the piano, to us)* By junior year, my grades were in the toilet. So they took me out of my school and sent me to a private one in another city. It felt like being sent to oblivion.

*(A smaller, quieter school. A bell. TEEN JEN, sixteen, walks the hall with her head down and talks to no one. At night, in her room: a rack of blinking modems and a phone with a headset. It rings. She answers on the first ring, all business.)*

TEEN JEN: Tech support. *(a beat)* Is it plugged in?

JENNIFER: *(to us)* I'd started an internet service provider at the end of junior high. That year, it was the only thing that was mine.

*(Senior year. It's an open campus, and when the last bell rings TEEN JEN is the first one out the door. On her way out she passes YOUNG NORA, seventeen, at a locker. Neither of them stops.)*

JENNIFER: *(to us)* Nora went to that school too, my senior year. We barely said a word to each other.

*(After school, across the stage: YOUNG NORA at a folding table under a hand-lettered sign, with a phone and a stack of pamphlets. The phone rings. She picks it up and listens, carefully. TEEN JEN never sees.)*

*(An empty classroom after the bell. TEEN JEN at a desk with a spiral notebook. On the board: ENGLISH. DUE FRIDAY.)*

{{song: 06 - The Notebook}}

---

### Scene 7: The Assignment

*Senior year, spring. A bench a few blocks from the school, as far away as a free period allows. TEEN JEN sits alone with the spiral notebook, which has gotten fatter. She reads her poem aloud to nobody.*

{{song: 07 - Road Less Traveled}}

*(She tears the page out, folds it once, and drops it onto a stack of papers on a teacher's empty desk. It disappears among the others.)*

JENNIFER: *(to us)* I turned it in. Nobody asked which road. *(a beat)* It took me a long time to answer it anyway.

---

### Scene 8: June Snow

*A riverbank near campus, June 2001. Cottonwood fluff drifts through the light like snow. JENNIFER, in college now, comes down the path in clothes she picked out herself. A JOGGER passes.*

JOGGER: Morning.

JENNIFER: *(a beat late, delighted)* Morning.

{{song: 08 - June Snow}}

---

### Scene 9: The Party

*That fall. A house party: a kitchen packed too tight, a keg in the sink, a birthday banner for somebody's roommate. JENNIFER, a margarita in her hand, is laughing with her friends. She's dressed somewhere in between, and in public she goes by Jen; these friends know the deal. LEAH, in a flannel over a T-shirt, leans on the counter, watching the room like she's taking notes.*

{{song: 09 - The Party}}

*(Later. The party is thinning out. JENNIFER, still in LEAH's flannel, and LEAH by the door.)*

LEAH: Keep the shirt.

JENNIFER: I'll give it back.

LEAH: I know. That's the point.

---

### Scene 10: Giving Up

*Thanksgiving break, 2001. The Whitcomb kitchen. The linen napkins are out for tomorrow.*

{{song: 10 - Giving Up}}

JENNIFER: *(from the piano, to us)* Fifteen years.

---

### Scene 11: Leah and the Boys

*Fifteen years go by in a few minutes. The house fills up: a crib, a dog bed, school shoes by the door. The coat hook by the door is empty, because JEN is wearing the coat.*

{{song: 11 - Leah and the Boys}}

---

### Scene 12: Another Night

*February 2016. The house, late. Everyone else is asleep. JEN sits at the kitchen table in the sport coat, with a stack of bills and a cold cup of coffee. The phone rings.*

{{song: 12 - Another Night}}

---

### Scene 13: The Bedside

JENNIFER: *(from the piano, to us)* "It's now," I thought. Then it was October.

*(October 2016. A doctor's office. LEAH and JEN in two chairs, holding hands across the gap. A DOCTOR says a word we don't hear. LEAH nods like she already knew.)*

LEAH: Don't tell the boys yet.

JEN: Okay.

LEAH: And don't look at me like that. I'm not dead.

JEN: I'm not looking at you like anything.

LEAH: You've always been a terrible liar.

*(A beat. They both hear it.)*

{{song: 13 - Fluorescent Moon}}

---

### Scene 14: Two Worlds

*That winter. Morning. Down the hall, LEAH sleeps in the hospital bed. JEN stands at the kitchen sink with a pharmacy bag. She reads the label twice, runs the tap, and takes the first one. Then she stands there with both hands on the edge of the sink.*

LEAH: *(from the bedroom)* You up? Is there coffee?

JEN: *(steady)* Coming.

{{song: 14 - Ghost in the Machine}}

---

### Scene 15: Again

*The Whitcomb living room, that same winter. Linen coasters under the coffee cups. DR. and MRS. WHITCOMB on the sofa. JENNIFER standing, in the coat.*

JENNIFER: I need to tell you something. I've told you before.

MRS. WHITCOMB: *(already knowing)* Oh.

{{song: 15 - The Winter Coat}}

---

### Scene 16: The Funeral

*April 2017. A small chapel: folding chairs, lilies, a framed photo of LEAH laughing. RUTH in her robes. JENNIFER in the front row with OWEN, twelve, and THEO, two. Across the aisle sit the WHITCOMBS and CLAIRE, who don't sit with her.*

{{song: 16 - Ruth's Blessing}}

---

### Scene 17: The Club

*Not long after. The phone. DR. WHITCOMB.*

DR. WHITCOMB: The club's having its spring dinner. Your mother and I would like you there. *(a beat)* Wear a jacket.

*(JENNIFER looks down at the coat, still hanging open. She buttons it, all the way up, one last time.)*

{{song: 17 - Sport Coat at the Door}}

---

### Scene 18: Hal

*JENNIFER at a laptop, late at night. A DOCTOR's voice, from somewhere:* "Any family history we should know about?" *She doesn't know. She starts looking.*

*Across the stage, a phone rings. HAL answers.*

HAL: Hello?

JENNIFER: Hi. This is going to sound strange. I think you might be my father.

*(A long pause.)*

HAL: Is this about a kidney? Because I've only got the two.

*(JENNIFER laughs before she can stop herself. So does he: the same laugh.)*

{{song: 18 - Turns Out It's Genetic}}

---

### Scene 19: The Photograph

*That night. JENNIFER at her laptop, Hal's photo still on the screen. She looks at it for a long time. Then she opens Facebook and types a name.*

{{song: 19 - The Photograph}}

{{song: 20 - Small Euphorias}}

### Prologue

*A house, open like a dollhouse: rooms on two levels, every window dark. Center, a kitchen table with more chairs than it needs. Downstage, an upright piano with a laptop on it and a mug of tea gone cold. On the stage floor, painted white on black like a road marking: a long box and two words, KEEP CLEAR. By the front door, an empty coat hook.*

*Somewhere in the house, a music box plays.*

*JENNIFER, now, comes in and sits at the piano. For a moment she looks at the two chairs nearest the door. Then she picks out the music-box tune on the keys.*

#### ♪ I Left the Lights On (Prologue)

*(A music box plays four bars and stops. JENNIFER finds the same notes on the piano, slower.)*  

**JENNIFER**  
I know you heard a story,  
and you were young when it was told,  
and you needed something steady  
and a story you could hold.  
I won't stand up and fight it.  
I won't call anybody wrong.  
I just wrote down what happened,  
and I put it in a song.  

I don't know what to tell you.  
I've never known the way.  
So I wrote down every year of it,  
every word I couldn't say.  
It's all here when you're ready.  
Play them out of order, too.  
I'm not asking you to believe me,  
just to listen till it's through.  

*(She reaches up and switches on a lamp. One window of the house glows.)*  

*(spoken)*  
Come inside.

### Scene 1: The Gawkers

*A train whistle, far off. The light changes: headlights, a crossing signal. The COMPANY drifts on as GAWKERS, slowing down the way you slow down for a wreck: commuters, a nurse on her break, a teenager filming on a phone. At the edge of it all stands a man in a short-sleeved dress shirt with a clipboard. This is THE ADJUSTER. He writes things down. He will be at the edge of a lot of scenes.*

JENNIFER: *(to the audience)* Oh, don't be shy. You paid for the good seats.

#### ♪ Trainwreck

*(A train-beat shuffle kicks in.)*  

**JENNIFER**  
The insurance said I'm fit for work and sent me back to grind.  
Turns out I'd had the cancer for years. It must've slipped their mind.  
Now a man up on the Rock is watching me like I'm a racketeer,  
and we sold the house for pennies. Wasn't that a banner year?  
Chased a promise up the coast and landed on my face,  
and my play count doubled. Guess you like a basket case.  

Every time I hit the ground,  
the numbers come around...  

Everybody slows down for a trainwreck,  

**COMPANY**  
*(Slow down!)*  

**JENNIFER**  
rolls the window down to see,  
and I don't mind you looking.  
I've made a catalog of me.  
Hit repeat, hit share, hit like,  
pour a whiskey, take a seat.  
My worst year's my biggest hit,  
and the encore's on repeat.  

Wrote a song about my dying, and somebody left a heart,  
and a guy in Ohio asked me if I'd sell him the art.  
The algorithm loves me now. I finally found my niche:  
turns out nothing trends like a woman in a ditch.  
The chatbot says to call a hotline. I said, "Honey, I'm all right.  
It's not a breakdown, it's a musical, and it opens here tonight."  

Every time I hit the ground,  

**COMPANY**  
the numbers come around...  

**JENNIFER and COMPANY**  
Everybody slows down for a trainwreck,  
rolls the window down to see,  
and I don't mind you looking.  
I've made a catalog of me.  
Hit repeat, hit share, hit like,  
pour a whiskey, take a seat.  
My worst year's my biggest hit,  
and the encore's on repeat.  

*(Instrumental break: hot fiddle, then steel. The GAWKERS hold up their phones. THE ADJUSTER writes.)*  

**JENNIFER** *(voice and pedal steel only; she finds someone out in the dark of the house)*  
But I see you there in the dark, hon.  
You're not here to point and stare.  
You've been riding this train too, friend.  
Come sit down. I saved a chair.  
We'll wave at all the gawkers

You hold on to your ticket,

**JENNIFER and COMPANY** *(full band, rowdy)*  
Everybody slows down for a trainwreck,

there's room in the caboose.  
My worst year's my biggest hit,

**JENNIFER** *(spoken, deadpan)*  
Next stop: the beginning.  
Y'all hold on.

### Scene 2: Linen Napkins

*The Whitcomb dining room, years ago: white tablecloth, silver, linen napkins in rings. DR. WHITCOMB, a pediatrician, eats with precision. MRS. WHITCOMB sits very straight. Between them sits YOUNG JEN, about nine, in a navy blazer one size too big.*

JENNIFER: *(from the piano)* Seventeen and scared, she gave me away. That's everything I know about the first part. The second part had a dress code.

YOUNG JEN: Dad. Why did the—

DR. WHITCOMB: Elbows.

YOUNG JEN: *(elbows off)* Why did the pediatrician bring a ladder to work?

*(Nothing.)*

YOUNG JEN: Because all his patients were having growth spurts.

*(Nothing. MRS. WHITCOMB touches her napkin to her lips.)*

MRS. WHITCOMB: That's enough, sweetheart.

#### ♪ The Mirror

*(The joke has just died at the dinner table. YOUNG JEN folds her napkin very neatly.)*  

**JENNIFER** *(from the piano, to us)*  
I grew up in a house with linen napkins,  
where you laughed politely or you didn't laugh at all,  
and I'd crack a joke at Sunday dinner  
and watch it die against the wall.  

*(Later. MRS. WHITCOMB's dressing table. YOUNG JEN, eleven now, alone at the mirror.)*  

There's a boy in all the photographs  
in a collar buttoned tight,  
a crooked grin for every camera,  
standing still in the flash of light.  
But she'd sit at her mother's mirror,  
try a new name on for size,  

*(YOUNG JEN uncaps a lipstick and writes on the glass: JENNIFER.)*  

**YOUNG JEN** *(barely)*  
Jennifer.  

**JENNIFER**  
then wipe it off before the door clicked  
and put the boy back in her eyes.  

*(A door clicks somewhere. She wipes the mirror with her sleeve.)*  

You can fold a life up smaller.  
You can learn to sit and wait.  
But the walls were never strong enough,  
so she'd wander to the gate.  

There once was a boy who was always a girl.  
She just needed the sky to let her wings unfurl,  
'cause you can't cage a gender  
any more than you can cage a ghost.  
She'll slip between the bars you built  
to the place she's wanted most.  

*(DR. WHITCOMB in the doorway.)*  

**DR. WHITCOMB**  
Square your shoulders, son.  

**JENNIFER**  
So she learned to play the part.  

*(MRS. WHITCOMB buttons YOUNG JEN's blazer, every button, all the way up.)*  

Folded up her wings beneath a heavy coat,  
buttoned all the way to the heart.  
And she drifted down the hallways  
like a stranger in her skin,  
half a ghost inside her own life,  
just waiting to begin.  

And the world will tell you who to be.  
It'll bolt and chain the gate.  
But the gate was never strong enough.  
She'd be through it, soon or late.  

**JENNIFER and YOUNG JEN**  
There once was a boy who was always a girl.  
She just needed the sky to let her wings unfurl—  

*(The music stops short. YOUNG JEN alone, to the mirror.)*  

**YOUNG JEN**  
Jennifer.  

*(She checks the door. Then she does up the top button herself.)*

### Scene 3: The One Who Always Came

*Two short honks of a car horn outside. YOUNG JEN is at the front door before the second one. In comes LOUISE, Dr. Whitcomb's mother, in a cardigan and sensible shoes, with her car keys in one hand and a foil-covered pie tin in the other.*

LOUISE: That traffic nearly killed me. Who's hungry?

YOUNG JEN: Grandma. Why did the pediatrician bring a ladder to work?

LOUISE: Oh, for Pete's sake. Why?

YOUNG JEN: Because all his patients were having growth spurts.

*(LOUISE laughs: a big, helpless, Midwestern laugh, the first laugh anybody has had in this house. YOUNG JEN stares at her like she's never heard the sound before.)*

LOUISE: Tell me another one.

#### ♪ Louise

**JENNIFER**  
There once was a woman, her name was Louise,  
a Wisconsin girl who did as she pleased.  
At the hardware store she ran a tidy floor:  
"Use the spittoon, boys. That's what it's for."  
In a little town where the neighbors all knew  
if you picked your nose, and if you farted, too,  
she was tough as a fence post and warm as the sun,  
and somehow she made room for everyone.  

She'd come down from Sacramento in her Oldsmobile Ciera,  
white-knuckled on the freeway, the last one of her era.  

**LOUISE**  
That traffic nearly killed me!  

**JENNIFER**  
And she'd hug you at the door,  
and the house got loud and warm the way it never was before.  
She'd sit with me for hours, and she'd tell the same old jokes,  
the Ole and Lena ones she swore were true about her folks.  

She never broke, and she never gave in.  
She carried her sorrow underneath her skin.  
She'd say Walt was waiting, just like he said he'd be,  
and thirty-two years was nothing to eternity.  
She'd tell you she was fine, then she'd bake you a pie,  
and make room for one more and never ask why.  
She spent her whole life giving love away,  
and I still hear her voice when I think of her today.  

*(Sunday. A church pew. LOUISE in her good coat; YOUNG JEN beside her, swinging her feet.)*  

Every Sunday at her place, I'd sit beside her in the pew.  
I didn't understand it all, the things she swore were true.  
She believed that God was waiting, and her Walt was waiting too,  
so I went because she wanted me there.  

**LOUISE** *(holding out her hand to YOUNG JEN)*  
Come on, kiddo.  

**JENNIFER**  
That's what you do.  

**JENNIFER and LOUISE**  
She'd say Walt was waiting, just like he said he'd be,  
and thirty-two years was nothing to eternity.  


*(LOUISE gathers her keys. The car door; two short honks, going away. YOUNG JEN waves at the window until the car is gone.)*

JENNIFER: *(to us)* She was the one who always came.

YOUNG NORA: You're not dancing either.

TEEN JEN: I'm holding up the wall.

YOUNG NORA: Want help?

*(They hold up the wall together.)*

**TEEN JEN** *(small, to herself, while YOUNG NORA leans on the wall beside her)*  
We were thirteen at Danny's party,

**JENNIFER** *(from the piano)*  
We had a little while, maybe,

### Scene 5: Keep Clear

*A high school hallway between classes: lockers slamming, a bell about to ring. TEEN JEN, fourteen, at a locker. Down the hall, CASEY, Teen Jen's best friend, cups her hands around her mouth.*

CASEY: Jennifer!

*(TEEN JEN turns on a dime. Then she freezes. Half the hallway is looking at her. She's shocked, and embarrassed, and trying very hard not to grin.)*

TEEN JEN: *(hissing)* Are you crazy?

CASEY: You turned around.

JENNIFER: *(from the piano, to us)* First time I ever heard it out loud. I turned on a dime.

*(RUTH's kitchen: warm light, a table with a wobble, a cookie jar shaped like a cow. This is the first room in the show where nobody is performing. RUTH, Casey's mother and a minister, is at the stove. CASEY and TEEN JEN come in and drop their backpacks.)*

RUTH: You're staying for dinner.

CASEY: She always stays for dinner.

RUTH: Then she's always staying for dinner.

JENNIFER: *(to us)* I went to Ruth's house a lot. She believed in me. It was the one house where I didn't have to be anybody.

*(Another day. The KEEP CLEAR box on the floor lights up. Outside the high school, afternoon. Two lanes of stopped traffic, suggested by headlights. TEEN JEN, backpack on one shoulder, looks at the stopped cars and steps into the gap.)*

**TEEN JEN**

### Scene 6: Exile

JENNIFER: *(from the piano, to us)* By junior year, my grades were in the toilet. So they took me out of my school and sent me to a private one in another city. It felt like being sent to oblivion.

*(A smaller, quieter school. A bell. TEEN JEN, sixteen, walks the hall with her head down and talks to no one. At night, in her room: a rack of blinking modems and a phone with a headset. It rings. She answers on the first ring, all business.)*

TEEN JEN: Tech support. *(a beat)* Is it plugged in?

JENNIFER: *(to us)* I'd started an internet service provider at the end of junior high. That year, it was the only thing that was mine.

*(Senior year. It's an open campus, and when the last bell rings TEEN JEN is the first one out the door. On her way out she passes YOUNG NORA, seventeen, at a locker. Neither of them stops.)*

JENNIFER: *(to us)* Nora went to that school too, my senior year. We barely said a word to each other.

*(After school, across the stage: YOUNG NORA at a folding table under a hand-lettered sign, with a phone and a stack of pamphlets. The phone rings. She picks it up and listens, carefully. TEEN JEN never sees.)*

*(An empty classroom after the bell. TEEN JEN at a desk with a spiral notebook. On the board: ENGLISH. DUE FRIDAY.)*

#### ♪ The Notebook

**TEEN JEN** *(writing)*  
Some things can't be known,  
and some things can't be said,  
not because they won't allow it,  
but the words stay in my head.  
I'm the only one I know of  
who is built the way I'm built,  
and I don't know what to call it,  
so I carry it like guilt.  

'Cause words can't ever say  
just why I feel this way.  
I'm torn in two, and I don't know who  
would listen anyway.  
No, words can't ever say  
just why I feel this way.  

*(She looks out the window. The piano's left hand starts to pulse.)*  

**TEEN JEN**  
And the longer that I wait,  
the more this feeling grows.  
It tells me, take to the air and fly,  
and where to, no one knows.  

What I'd give to slip away,  
to fly up and up into the azure sky!  
What I'd give to leave today,  
to leave this ground and fly!  

My wings give out. I fall. I try.  
I disappear into the sky.  
And the sky goes on forever, but it only lets you in  
if you've got the nerve to fly.  

What I'd give to slip away...  

**JENNIFER** *(from the piano, over her)*  
Oh, how as time went by,  
that need that told me, fly,  
just grew and grew and wouldn't go.  
I'm thankful now I learned to fly,  
even if, for years and years,  
it was only in my mind.  

*(A bell. TEEN JEN shuts the notebook and is out the door before it stops ringing.)*

### Scene 7: The Assignment

*Senior year, spring. A bench a few blocks from the school, as far away as a free period allows. TEEN JEN sits alone with the spiral notebook, which has gotten fatter. She reads her poem aloud to nobody.*

#### ♪ Road Less Traveled

*(TEEN JEN reads from the notebook. The first verse is spoken over a few piano chords; the music gathers under her until she's singing.)*  

**TEEN JEN** *(reading)*  
Here I stand, on the precipice of eternity,  
gazing out across the vast expanse that is me,  
having to decide in one night, forever, who I will be,  
looking into the future to decide what to do with my past.  

*(sung)*  
Behind me lies the life of a man,  
morals intact, skills in demand.  
Ahead is a fork in the road,  
and I will take the road less traveled.  

Few mortals dare cross the threshold,  
dare approach the limits of tolerance,  
dare cross the line into the unknown,  
and this is where I must boldly go.  

The answer lies in plain view,  
but the question begs to be forgotten.  
To one side lies happiness at unspoken cost.  
On the other, innocence lost.  

Behind me lies the life of a man,  
morals intact, skills in demand.  
Ahead is a fork in the road,  
and I will take the road less traveled.  

*(The music swells: strings, a low drum.)*  

Wingèd time with its outstretched talons

*(She closes the notebook.)*  


*(She tears the page out, folds it once, and drops it onto a stack of papers on a teacher's empty desk. It disappears among the others.)*

JENNIFER: *(to us)* I turned it in. Nobody asked which road. *(a beat)* It took me a long time to answer it anyway.

### Scene 8: June Snow

*A riverbank near campus, June 2001. Cottonwood fluff drifts through the light like snow. JENNIFER, in college now, comes down the path in clothes she picked out herself. A JOGGER passes.*

JOGGER: Morning.

JENNIFER: *(a beat late, delighted)* Morning.

#### ♪ June Snow

**JENNIFER**  
Every day when I go out,  
the cottonwoods dance for me.  
They drift across the air  
as light as light can be.  
The birds pick up the melody.  
The rabbits hop in tune.  
The river chimes in, rippling,  
and it's not even noon.  

The cottonwoods dance for me!  
They float across my skin  
like a fine, warm snow in June.  
Some settle down to rest a while,  
and then they dance again.  

The trees sway along softly.  
The flowers do their part.  
And the sweetness of the morning  
goes straight into my heart.  
A boy from my econ class  
says, "Hey, Jen," and goes,  
like it's nothing, like it's Tuesday,  
like everybody knows.  

The cottonwoods dance for me!  
They float across my skin  
like a fine, warm snow in June.  
Some settle down to rest a while,  
and then they dance again.  

*(She stops on the footbridge and looks up.)*  

I look up at the azure sky  
I used to wish that I could fly to,  
and the clouds are watching too,  
as intoxicated as I.  

The cottonwoods dance for me!  
They float across my skin  
like a fine, warm snow in June,  
and every time they settle,  
they get up and dance again.  

*(spoken, as the music thins)*  
I spend an hour every day watching the dance.  
Then I go home.  
Invigorated. Intoxicated.  

*(A party flyer in her hand.)*  

I look forward to tomorrow's show.

### Scene 10: Giving Up

*Thanksgiving break, 2001. The Whitcomb kitchen. The linen napkins are out for tomorrow.*

#### ♪ Giving Up

*(JENNIFER is still in LEAH's flannel. MRS. WHITCOMB stands at the counter, her back half turned. At the edge of the light, TEEN JEN holds the spiral notebook.)*  

**JENNIFER** *(spoken)*  
Mom. I go by Jennifer now.  

**MRS. WHITCOMB** *(spoken, after a long time)*  
Are you sure?  

**JENNIFER** *(spoken)*  
Since I was eleven.  

**MRS. WHITCOMB** *(spoken)*  
Your father and I are paying for that school.  

*(She turns back to the counter. That's the end of the conversation.)*  

**TEEN JEN** *(sung, reading from the notebook)*  
why wreck so many lives  
when it's over my own strife?  
why hike so many miles  
to find an absence of smiles?  

what can you really do  
when you're no longer you?  
what can you really do  
when no one sees the real you?  

*(LEAH's dorm room. LEAH and JENNIFER sit on the bed, not quite touching.)*  

**LEAH** *(spoken)*  
I like you. I do. I just don't get why you need all... this.  

**JENNIFER** *(spoken)*  
It isn't "this." It's me.

### Scene 38: The Notebook, Again

*A closet. NORA drags out a cardboard box that has been in every closet they've ever had and sets it down in front of JENNIFER. On the side, in old marker: SCHOOL.*

NORA: Keep, toss, or pretend we never saw it?

JENNIFER: *(already opening it)* Keep.

*(NORA leaves her to it.)*

#### ♪ Unspoken Cost

*(Felt piano alone. JENNIFER finds the spiral notebook. She opens it. TEEN JEN is suddenly at a school desk again, writing, three feet away.)*  

**JENNIFER**  
I found you in a notebook  
on the edge of the night,  
an English class assignment  
with the sources cited right.  
They asked for Yeats and Shakespeare,  
so you let them hold the door,  
and you slipped the truth right past them.  
Nobody looked for more.  

You said behind you stood a man  
with his morals all in place.  

**TEEN JEN** *(soft, not hearing her)*  
Behind me lies the life of a man...  

**JENNIFER**  
You never once called him "I," love.

One road was happiness, you wrote,  
the other, innocence lost,  

**TEEN JEN**  
To one side lies happiness at unspoken cost...

### Scene 39: Carry Me

*Night. JENNIFER at the table with the notebook closed in front of her. Her phone buzzes. Then again. Then again.*

JENNIFER: *(reading)* Ruth: "Called and got your voicemail. I'll keep calling." *(Buzz.)* Casey: "Mom says call her. Also, call me." *(Buzz.)* Nora's family, the group chat: "Sunday dinner. Have you eaten? Doesn't matter. Come eat." *(Buzz.)* Hal: a link, and "Don't open this at work." *(a beat)* I don't work.

*(She opens it anyway, and laughs: the same laugh as his.)*

*(She puts the phone face down and looks around at the empty chairs. She doesn't cry. She almost laughs.)*

#### ♪ Carry Me

*(JENNIFER at the table with the empty chairs. Around the edges of the stage, in their own pools of light, far away and close at once: NORA, RUTH, CASEY, NORA's family, HAL, and TESS, Jennifer's sister by blood. **JENNIFER**  
I was born to a mother who didn't choose me,  
signed away to strangers from the start.  
Then the ones I learned to call my own let go,  
and they took my children with my heart.  
There's no stone that bears their names,  
no grave to lay a flower on.  
How do you grieve the ones still breathing  
who decided you were gone?  

Then I married into kindness I had never known:  
a table with a chair already set.  
No one asked me where I came from,  
just how I'd been, and had I eaten yet.  

**JENNIFER, NORA, RUTH, CASEY and NORA'S FAMILY**

### Scene 40: The Lights

*Evening. NORA sets the table: two plates for the two of them, and two more at the chairs by the door. She doesn't say anything about it. Far upstage, at the very edge of the light, THE LANDLORD waits with his ledger. Nobody looks at him. JENNIFER goes to the front door, opens it, and props it open.*

NORA: Leave it?

JENNIFER: Leave it.

#### ♪ I Left the Lights On (Finale)

*(JENNIFER and NORA sit side by side. Every other chair is empty: Louise's, Leah's, the small one for Hope, the Whitcombs', Claire's, and the two by the door, set with plates: Owen's and Theo's. The hook by the door is empty. A few soft celesta notes, like the music box.)*  

**JENNIFER** *(to the two chairs by the door)*  
It was never your fault, not any of it.  
You were never too much to keep.  
And I never stopped loving you, not for a day,  
not awake and not asleep.  
They took the chance to tell you,  
so I said it the only way:  
one song at a time, and I left them here  
for whenever you find your way.  

And you won't have to knock.  
There'll be nobody home.  
But I left the lights on  
in every song I wrote.  
Whatever you were told,  
whatever you decide,  
it's all still here, love.  
Come inside.  

*(Louise's melody, from #3. NORA hums it under her.)*  

She'd say Walt was waiting, just like he said he'd be,  
and thirty-two years was nothing to eternity.  
Well, I'm waiting too, as long as I'm allowed,  
and when I'm not, the songs will wait.  
They're waiting now.  

*(Strings out. Voice and piano.)*  

I know what it is to knock on a door  
and hear nobody come,  
so I built you a house out of all that I was,  
and I left every light on.  

*(Strings return, warm, not loud. One by one, the windows of the house light up. YOUNG JEN and TEEN JEN appear in two of them.)*  

**JENNIFER and NORA**  
So you won't have to knock.  
There'll be nobody home.  
But I left the lights on  
in every song I wrote.  
If you came late, you're not too late.  
Whatever you decide,  
it's all still here, love.  
Come inside.  

*(The music box plays the lullaby. JENNIFER sings it, very simply, to the two empty chairs. NORA takes the harmony.)*  

**JENNIFER**  
Little train, little train,  
running through the night,  
past the hill and past the river,  
past the last streetlight.  

**JENNIFER and NORA**  
When you wake up, I'll be here.  
When you're grown, you'll still be mine.  
I'll leave a light on at the station.  
Follow the line.  

*(Piano alone.)*  

**JENNIFER**  
The lights are on.  
Come in.  

*(Instead of a blackout, the house lights come up, slowly, all the way, on the audience. The stage stays lit too. Nobody turns anything off. The door stays open.)*  


**END OF PLAY**

### About this story  

It’s true. The names are changed; what happened isn’t. You may trim, cut, reorder and restage to make it work in your theater. What I ask you to keep:  

- **Jennifer was always a girl.** Don’t soften it, hide it, or turn it into a debate.  
- **Her boys were taken while she was sick, not because she was dangerous.** She owns her mistakes; let her. But don’t make her the villain, and don’t make the people who took them into cartoons either. “I won’t call anybody wrong. I just wrote down what happened.”  
- **She is dying, and she wants to choose her ending.** Don’t turn that into something she needs rescuing from.  
- **The family that carried her was chosen, not blood.** Nora stays at the center.  
- **It’s funny.** The jokes sit right next to the grief, because that’s how she lived it.  
- **The lights stay on at the end, and the door stays open.**  
- There's always a chance that new technology/research can change the course of things, but as it stands in 2026 she is incurable and several years into a relatively short expected lifespan for iodine refractory metastatic papillary thyroid cancer.

# Ruth's Blessing

**Act One · #16** · Sung by RUTH, JENNIFER  
**Status:** Stage draft 1  
**Source:** none (new). It's built on 1 Corinthians 13 (King James Version, public domain), the reading heard at both funerals and weddings.

**Plan:** NEW. Ruth leads Leah's funeral. A short hymn.

<!-- lyrics -->

*(JENNIFER holds LEAH's flannel shirt, folded.)*

**RUTH** *(spoken, reading)*
For now we see through a glass, darkly; but then face to face: now I know in part; but then shall I know even as also I am known.

*(sung, a plain hymn)*
We see in part, we know in part,
as through a darkened glass,
but someday, face to face, the heart
will know, when this has passed.

Go now in peace, and take your rest,
and set your burden down.
What you saw darkly, you'll see best
when you are homeward bound.

*(JENNIFER steps forward and lays the folded flannel on the coffin.)*

**JENNIFER** *(spoken, very quietly)*
I said I'd give it back.

*(OWEN takes THEO's hand. Back at the house, LEAH's chair at the table goes empty: the second, after Louise's.)*

**RUTH and JENNIFER**
We see in part, we know in part,
as through a darkened glass,
but someday, face to face, the heart
will know, when this has passed.

## Notes for the composer

- A real hymn: common meter (8.6.8.6), four-part harmony, singable by a congregation. It should sound like it's been in a hymnal for a hundred years.
- Unaccompanied, or a single organ or piano.

## What changed from the record

- This is a new song, so all the lines are mine, built on 1 Corinthians 13:12. That's the "through a glass, darkly... face to face" passage, read at funerals.
- "Through a glass, darkly" picks up the mirror from #2. "Face to face" sets up The Crossing (#29), where Leah finally sees her.
- Returning the flannel pays off #9 ("I'll give it back." "I know. That's the point.").
- OWEN is twelve and THEO two in April 2017. Owen turns thirteen later that year; he leaves in early 2018, before his next birthday.

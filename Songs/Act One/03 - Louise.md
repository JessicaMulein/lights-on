# Louise

**Act One · #3** · Sung by JENNIFER, LOUISE  
**Status:** Stage draft 1  
**Source:** an earlier song of yours (verses 1 and 4, chorus, bridge)

**Plan:** Dad's mother, the one who always came. Trim to verses plus the bridge; plant "thirty-two years was nothing."

<!-- lyrics -->

**JENNIFER**
There once was a woman, her name was Louise,
a Wisconsin girl who did as she pleased.
At the hardware store she ran a tidy floor:
"Use the spittoon, boys. That's what it's for."
In a little town where the neighbors all knew
if you picked your nose, and if you farted, too,
she was tough as a fence post and warm as the sun,
and somehow she made room for everyone.

She'd come down from Sacramento in her Oldsmobile Ciera,
white-knuckled on the freeway, the last one of her era.

**LOUISE**
That traffic nearly killed me!

**JENNIFER**
And she'd hug you at the door,
and the house got loud and warm the way it never was before.
She'd sit with me for hours, and she'd tell the same old jokes,
the Ole and Lena ones she swore were true about her folks.

She never broke, and she never gave in.
She carried her sorrow underneath her skin.
She'd say Walt was waiting, just like he said he'd be,
and thirty-two years was nothing to eternity.
She'd tell you she was fine, then she'd bake you a pie,
and make room for one more and never ask why.
She spent her whole life giving love away,
and I still hear her voice when I think of her today.

*(Sunday. A church pew. LOUISE in her good coat; YOUNG JEN beside her, swinging her feet.)*

Every Sunday at her place, I'd sit beside her in the pew.
I didn't understand it all, the things she swore were true.
She believed that God was waiting, and her Walt was waiting too,
so I went because she wanted me there.

**LOUISE** *(holding out her hand to YOUNG JEN)*
Come on, kiddo.

**JENNIFER**
That's what you do.

**JENNIFER and LOUISE**
She'd say Walt was waiting, just like he said he'd be,
and thirty-two years was nothing to eternity.

## Notes for the composer

- Americana story-song: piano, fiddle, upright bass, a little accordion if the band has one. Warm and Midwestern, with a laugh in it.
- "and thirty-two years was nothing to eternity" is the most important phrase in the number. Give it its own melody, because the finale (#40) brings it back when Jennifer is the one waiting.
- Louise's final verse (the phone call, "I don't think I can make it another night") is held for her reprise, #12 Another Night, in February 2016.

## What changed from the record

- Stage names: the woman in the song is Louise and her husband is Walt. The hardware store and the town aren't named.
- Opening rhyme: the original rhymed on her real name; this one is "Louise / pleased."
- The car is now the Oldsmobile Ciera (the model's actual spelling), rhymed with "era."
- Kept verse 1, verse 4 (the drives), the chorus and the bridge. Cut verse 2 (the Army and meeting Walt, which the chorus carries), verse 3 (her advocacy and the nursing-home visits), and verse 5 and the final verse, which are held for the reprise.
- Chorus: "bake you some pie / his memory in her head" became "bake you a pie / and never ask why," a true rhyme. "made room for everyone" now echoes in the chorus too.
- The bridge is rewritten to rhyme (pew / true / too / do) while keeping your lines and its ending: "That's what you do."
- Walt died in 1984, so "thirty-two years" puts Louise's death in February 2016, which is #12.

**Status:** Stage draft 1

## Notes for the composer

- Early-2000s indie folk-pop: acoustic guitar, brushed drums, a little Wurlitzer. Giddy, around 112 BPM.
- "Say it again" is the hook. Write it so it can come back slowed and in a minor key. It can return in The Crossing (#29) if, on the far shore, Leah finally says the name.
- Leah's questions are quick and direct, nearly spoken. Jennifer's answers get more musical as she relaxes.
- The flannel echoes the too-big flannel Teen Jen wore at Danny's party (#4). It folds up in #10, and it could come back at Leah's funeral (#16).

## What changed from the record

- In public she goes by "Jen," the in-between name, the way you went by "Jess." Her friends at the party know the deal, and one of them calls her "Jennifer" in passing; that's how Leah hears it. The friend's line is mine.
- This is a new song; all the lines are mine. You remembered wearing her shirt. The spilled drink, Leah's questions and "Say it again" are invented, so change anything that rings false.
- It keeps the outline's promise: Leah starts out curious, and the narration admits she "didn't always like what I said."

# Giving Up

**Act One · #10** · Sung by TEEN JEN, JENNIFER / JEN (MRS. WHITCOMB and LEAH, spoken)  
**Status:** Stage draft 1  
**Source:** "Giving Up"

**Plan:** Telling her mother, who is paying for school. Leah curious, then not supportive. The teen poem returns as prophecy; the coat goes back on for fifteen years.

<!-- lyrics -->

*(JENNIFER is still in LEAH's flannel. MRS. WHITCOMB stands at the counter, her back half turned. At the edge of the light, TEEN JEN holds the spiral notebook.)*

**JENNIFER** *(spoken)*
Mom. I go by Jennifer now.

**MRS. WHITCOMB** *(spoken, after a long time)*
Are you sure?

**JENNIFER** *(spoken)*
Since I was eleven.

**MRS. WHITCOMB** *(spoken)*
Your father and I are paying for that school.

*(She turns back to the counter. That's the end of the conversation.)*

**TEEN JEN** *(sung, reading from the notebook)*
why wreck so many lives
when it's over my own strife?
why hike so many miles
to find an absence of smiles?

what can you really do
when you're no longer you?
what can you really do
when no one sees the real you?

*(LEAH's dorm room. LEAH and JENNIFER sit on the bed, not quite touching.)*

**LEAH** *(spoken)*
I like you. I do. I just don't get why you need all... this.

**JENNIFER** *(spoken)*
It isn't "this." It's me.

**LEAH** *(spoken)*
Couldn't you just be you, without it?

**TEEN JEN**
why does no one understand?
even the most caring never can.
why does it have to be
just family, or just me?

what can you really do
when there are none exactly like you?
what can you really do
when saving family means throwing away you?

*(JENNIFER alone. She takes off LEAH's flannel and folds it, carefully. On the back of a chair hangs the navy blazer from Act One, grown up into a man's sport coat.)*

**TEEN JEN and JENNIFER**
why must the end always be so near
when you're living a life of fear?

*(She puts the coat on and buttons it, all the way up. From here until #15 she is JEN.)*

**TEEN JEN**
what can you really do
when your life is through?

**JEN**
what can you really do
when you have to leave the dock
in a leaky canoe?

*(LEAH comes in, sees the coat, and smiles, relieved. JEN smiles back. It costs her everything.)*

<!-- /lyrics -->

## Notes for the composer

- Like Road Less Traveled, this stays a teenager's poem, so it keeps its own rhymes. Give Teen Jen a plain, almost nursery-simple tune, repeated, against long silences in the scene.
- No band; piano and a single cello. Jennifer's voice joins Teen Jen's only at the bridge, the moment she gives in.

## What changed from the record

- The poem is nearly verbatim. "when just over my own strifes" became "when it's over my own strife," "when living in a life of fear" became "when you're living a life of fear," and "leave the docks in leaky canoe" became "leave the dock / in a leaky canoe," all for singing.
- Mrs. Whitcomb's and Leah's spoken lines are placeholders I wrote. If you remember what was actually said, use that instead.
- Mrs. Whitcomb's "Are you sure?" sets up #15, fifteen years later, where it could just be "Again?"

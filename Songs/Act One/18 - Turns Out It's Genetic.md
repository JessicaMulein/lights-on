## Notes for the composer

- Playful, wry, piano-led folk-pop: bouncy barroom piano, upright bass, brushed snare, handclaps, and a whistled hook. It's the first real duet of joy in the show.
- Hal should be a baritone who can land a comic line.

## What changed from the record

- The linen-napkins verse moved to #2, so this opens with its second half as a callback.
- **New: the kibbutz.** After "I'd been looking for my people," Hal answers: "Your people? I was raised on a kibbutz. You've got a lot of people." The kibbutz is true; the rest of the line is mine. It turns "my people" into the moment you find out you're Jewish. If it doesn't sit right, it comes out cleanly.
- The photograph arrives the way it did: Hal emails it. It's a relative of yours on his side, a great-aunt or great-grandmother, and Hal knows exactly who she is. She looks like your wife (she agreed), which is what sends you to Facebook. "Thought you'd want this one. She's one of ours." is my wording.
- Hal sings now: "I'm an engineer, of course I am," and his own version of the chorus line ("I found my daughter late in life").
- "We talked tolerances and load / ...and I laughed so hard I nearly choked" became "tolerances and code / ...and my mascara overflowed," a true rhyme (and two engineers will hear the buffer overflow).
- "that ought to be encrypted / that's laughing at the script" became "where all of this was scripted," a true rhyme.
- The bridge's placeholder ("Took fifty years to get here. *(adjust)*") became "Took half my life to get here. / Postage due."
- Final chorus: "and I wouldn't have it any other way" became "and I'm laughing all the same," which rhymes with "shame."
- **New: your siblings.** After the Reddit verse, Tess, Maggie and Sam call in, and the song gets a second chorus about the other thing that turned out to be genetic: "and the whole family tree is gay." The intros are my words built on what you told me. Hal's dry "About half the time." makes him bi rather than gay. Your birth mother is still left out, as you wanted; if you'd ever like her in the joke, it can be "About half the time. Same as your mother."
- Tess's last line sets up your brother who was taken. "Maybe he changed it. People do." is my guess from yours; Carry Me (#39) leaves a light on for him.
- The bridge gains two lines: "And the part they tried to button up? / Same envelope." It calls back the buttoned-up blazer in The Mirror (#2), so the final chorus's "nurture gave me the shame" now means both things.
- The photograph is new. You said it was a photo from your birth father that reminded you of your wife. How it actually happened is yours to fill in.

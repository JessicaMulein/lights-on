**Status:** Stage draft 1

## Notes for the composer

- The record is dark and cinematic. Here, build it on a slow electronic pulse that sounds like a monitor, with low strings over it. It stays quiet; it's a sickroom.
- The lightbox is a visual cue, but give it a sound too: a faint high tone whenever it brightens. When it comes back in Act Two (#28), the audience will remember the sound before they remember the picture.

## What changed from the record

- Split between the two of them: Leah has the patient's lines ("It takes all of your energy"), and Jen has the caregiver's ("I count her breaths").
- "Counting breaths like numbers on a screen / between the person that you were" became "I count her breaths... between the person that she was and what I've seen."
- "The calendar pages lose their shape and blur, / a quiet war behind a closed hospital door" became "and the war is behind a closed door, and it's hers," a true rhyme with "blur."
- The bridge stays word for word, split between them. Its "unspoken price" quietly echoes Unspoken Cost.
- The record's title line, "Cancer is a bitch," goes to Jen at the very end, said to no one.
- New staging: the lightbox with her own scan, and the hospital bed moving home.
- The timeline follows what you told me: diagnosed around October 2016, eight months after Louise, with her November birthday known to be her last, and gone by April 2017. The birthday cake comes back in Frozen in the Light (#24).

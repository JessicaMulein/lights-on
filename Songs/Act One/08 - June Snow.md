# June Snow

**Act One · #8** · Sung by JENNIFER  
**Status:** Stage draft 1  
**Source:** "June Snow"

**Plan:** College, 2001: out as Jennifer for the first time. The cottonwoods become what freedom felt like. A full number.

**JENNIFER**
Every day when I go out,
the cottonwoods dance for me.
They drift across the air
as light as light can be.
The birds pick up the melody.
The rabbits hop in tune.
The river chimes in, rippling,
and it's not even noon.

The cottonwoods dance for me!
They float across my skin
like a fine, warm snow in June.
Some settle down to rest a while,
and then they dance again.

The trees sway along softly.
The flowers do their part.
And the sweetness of the morning
goes straight into my heart.
A boy from my econ class
says, "Hey, Jen," and goes,
like it's nothing, like it's Tuesday,
like everybody knows.

The cottonwoods dance for me!
They float across my skin
like a fine, warm snow in June.
Some settle down to rest a while,
and then they dance again.

*(She stops on the footbridge and looks up.)*

I look up at the azure sky
I used to wish that I could fly to,
and the clouds are watching too,
as intoxicated as I.

The cottonwoods dance for me!
They float across my skin
like a fine, warm snow in June,
and every time they settle,
they get up and dance again.

*(spoken, as the music thins)*
I spend an hour every day watching the dance.
Then I go home.
Invigorated. Intoxicated.

*(A party flyer in her hand.)*

I look forward to tomorrow's show.

## Notes for the composer

- Acoustic fingerpicking, then a cello or a woodwind in verse 2. Light and dancing, around 100 BPM. This is the happiest Jennifer is in the whole first act, and it should feel like stepping into the sun.
- "Every day when I go out" carries a double meaning now; let the first line land.
- Set "the azure sky" to the same notes as "to fly up and up into the azure sky" in The Notebook (#6). The teenager wished for that sky, and here she's standing under it.

## What changed from the record

- I've made this the song of your first season out, since it comes from the same time. If the cottonwoods were just cottonwoods, tell me, and we can find the freedom song somewhere else.
- Your images all stay: the dance, the birds, the rabbits, the river, the fine warm snow, the clouds, and the spoken ending.
- Verse 1 is tightened into rhymes (me / be, tune / noon).
- New lines, mine rather than yours: "as light as light can be"; the econ-class verse ("says, 'Hey, Jen,' and goes... like everybody knows"); and "I used to wish that I could fly to," which ties back to The Notebook.
- The last chorus changes its ending to "every time they settle, they get up and dance again."
- The party flyer hands off to #9.

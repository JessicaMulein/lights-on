# Leah and the Boys

**Act One · #11** · Sung by JEN, LEAH (OWEN, spoken), with JENNIFER  
**Status:** Stage draft 1  
**Source:** none (new), plus four lines of "The Glass Firewall"

**Plan:** NEW. The marriage, lived inside the coat. Owen (2004), Theo (2015) in his little train bed. The lullaby. Leah is neither villain nor saint.

<!-- lyrics -->

*(A small wedding. LEAH in white. JEN in the sport coat, buttoned all the way up.)*

**LEAH**
I wanted a house with a porch and a swing,
a husband, some kids, all the regular things.

**JEN**
And I wanted to give you every one.

**LEAH**
So give me every one.

*(An office. A conference table. JEN at the far end, in the coat. Men talk over her.)*

**JEN**
Too soft for the table, too quiet to count,
sitting right at the bottom of the corporate mount.
They passed me right over, they locked up the prize,
while the truth was a ghost shining straight through my eyes.

*(A hospital room, 2004. LEAH, exhausted and glowing, hands JEN a baby.)*

**LEAH** *(spoken)*
Say hi to your son.

*(JEN holds OWEN. The music box tune from the Prologue, very softly. She sings it to him, the first time anyone has sung it.)*

**JEN**
Little train, little train,
running through the night,
past the hill and past the river,
past the last streetlight.
When you wake up, I'll be here.
When you're grown, you'll still be mine.
I'll leave a light on at the station.
Follow the line.

*(The years go by in the house: birthdays, a dog, a mortgage. LEAH and JEN pass each other in the hallway, close and careful.)*

**JENNIFER** *(from the piano, now)*
She knew. She'd always known.
We never said it, not once, in all those years.
It sat in the corner of every house we lived in,
like a chair nobody used.

*(2015. A bedroom with a little bed shaped like a train engine. THEO, tiny, is in it. OWEN, eleven, reads in the corner and pretends not to listen. JEN sits on the floor between them.)*

**JEN**
Little train, little train,
running through the night,

**OWEN** *(spoken, not looking up)*
Past the hill and past the river.

**JEN**
past the last streetlight.

*(LEAH in the doorway, watching. She joins in, quietly, on the harmony.)*

**JEN and LEAH**
When you wake up, I'll be here.
When you're grown, you'll still be mine.
I'll leave a light on at the station.
Follow the line.

*(JEN switches on a nightlight. The music box plays the tune. Something unsaid passes between JEN and LEAH in the doorway. Then LEAH smiles, and turns off the hall light.)*

## Notes for the composer

- The lullaby is the music-box tune from the Prologue, and it's the most important melody in the show. Write it first. It needs to work as a toy (music box), as a lullaby (voice and guitar), broken (Frozen in the Light, #24), and full orchestra (the finale, #40).
- "I'll leave a light on at the station" plants the finale: the promise she made to the boys as babies is the one she keeps in "I Left the Lights On."
- The wedding and office sections are quick sketches, under a minute together. The marriage is real and affectionate, so play it warm. The tension is only in what's never said.

## What changed from the record

- This is a new song; all the lines are mine except the four from The Glass Firewall ("Too soft for the table...").
- I gave Leah one honest want: "a husband, some kids, all the regular things." It shows who she was without making her the villain, and the word "husband" does the damage on its own. Change it if it isn't true to her.
- The lullaby's train comes from Theo's train bed. Trains already run through the show (Trainwreck), and "Follow the line" doubles as a line of a song.

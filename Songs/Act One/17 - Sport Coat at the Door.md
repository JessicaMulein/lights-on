# Sport Coat at the Door

**Act One · #17** · Sung by JENNIFER, CLUB MEN (DR. WHITCOMB, a COAT CHECK ATTENDANT, spoken)  
**Status:** Stage draft 1  
**Source:** "Sport Coat at the Door," and one line of "The Cremation of Care"

**Plan:** After 2016. Dad's club still wants the coat; she leaves it at the door for good.

<!-- lyrics -->

*(The Pacific Union. Heavy wood, marble, oil-painted fathers. DR. WHITCOMB, at home here. JENNIFER beside him in the coat, buttoned all the way up, because he asked her to wear one.)*

**JENNIFER**
Heavy wood and marble floors, standing by those heavy doors,
Pacific Union with my dad, wearing clothes that made me sad.
I tried to check my burning coat. A sudden lump caught in my throat.

**COAT CHECK ATTENDANT** *(spoken)*
Only women do that here.

**JENNIFER**
They said it, and they made it clear.
So I put it back upon my frame, and walked right into the domain.

Caught between the tailored lines and where I really live,
holding onto secrets that I didn't want to give,
looking for a quiet room, a door that I could close,
to leave that heavy jacket where the coat check never goes.

*(Town and Country, visitors' day. Chintz, soft light, women at tea. MRS. WHITCOMB, guarded, with JENNIFER beside her.)*

Town and Country, visitors' day: beside my mother I found my way.
A women's club, a softer light. For the first time it felt so right.
I sank into the cushions there, breathing in the open air,
but kept it quiet, kept it small, afraid they'd see me after all.

Caught between the tailored lines and where I really live,
holding onto secrets that I didn't want to give,
looking for a quiet room, a door that I could close,
to leave that heavy jacket where the coat check never goes.

*(DR. WHITCOMB alone, in an armchair, looking at a photograph of redwoods.)*

I never made the trip up to the Bohemian Grove
to see the shadows hidden in that private redwood cove,
just a fleeting curiosity I never got to see,
a rite of passage meant for him, but never meant for me.

*(Outside the Pacific Union. JENNIFER takes off the coat, the one she has worn since #10, folds it over the brass rail by the door, and walks away without it.)*

**JENNIFER** *(spoken)*
Up north, they cremate care, and they keep the world.
I kept the care. I gave up the world.

*(sung, quietly)*
Some doors I never opened.
Some rooms I left behind.
I left that sport coat at the door.
I don't need it anymore.
I found my place.
I found my home.

## Notes for the composer

- This is the club sound from #2: stiff strings, a polite minuet. In Town and Country it softens, and by the outro it's down to piano.
- The verses are internal-rhymed couplets (floors/doors, dad/sad); keep the musical line long so the inner rhymes land.

## What changed from the record

- It now comes after she's out (2016). Her father asked her to wear the coat to his club, which is the point.
- "'Only women do that here,' they said and made the boundaries clear" is split: the coat-check attendant says the line, and Jennifer finishes, "They said it, and they made it clear."
- Chorus: "And leave that heavy jacket underneath the eaves" became "to leave that heavy jacket where the coat check never goes," which rhymes with "close" and ties back to the coat check.
- Verse 2: "But looking over my own shoulder, keeping quiet as I got older / Afraid they'd find my secret out, afraid to get too comfortable" became "but kept it quiet, kept it small, afraid they'd see me after all," a true couplet.
- From The Cremation of Care: "They cremate care up there and they keep the world. / I kept the care. I gave up the world." I trimmed it to two spoken lines before the outro.
- She leaves the coat on the club's brass rail. It's the same coat she has worn since #10: unbuttoned in #15, but never taken off until now. The hook by the door stays empty from here on.

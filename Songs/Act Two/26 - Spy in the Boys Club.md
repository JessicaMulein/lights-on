# Spy in the Boys Club

**Act Two · #26** · Sung by JENNIFER, OFFICE MEN  
**Status:** Stage draft 1  
**Source:** "Spy in the Boys Club"

**Plan:** The dream job. Optional comic lift.

<!-- lyrics -->

*(Seattle. A glass-walled conference room, a whiteboard, a bowl of free snacks. JENNIFER on her first day, with a badge. The OFFICE MEN snap their fingers on two and four; a slinky upright bass creeps underneath.)*

**JENNIFER**
I had the tailored uniform. I knew the secret knock.
I stood inside the inner circle, listening to the talk.
When the heavy doors were closed and the ladies left the room,
I saw the way you operate through the whiskey and the gloom.
I nodded at the punchlines. I memorized the code,
a perfect double agent on a heavy-shouldered road.

You handed me the keys to the kingdom of the men,
but I was planning my escape before the meeting could begin!

'Cause I was the spy in the boys' club, standing by the wall.
I saw the golden pedestal. I saw the way it falls.
I had the golden ticket, the unearned benefit,
but the armor was a cage, and the jacket didn't fit!
So I left my deep disguise hanging on the leather chair
and walked right out the fortress with the wind in my hair!

*(An OFFICE MAN takes the marker out of her hand and explains her own diagram back to her.)*

Now I'm standing on the outside, and it's funny how it goes:
you're explaining my own expertise directly to my nose.
I lost the automatic nod, the quiet right of way,
the VIP invisible pass I carried every day.
But baby, I don't miss it: the ego and the musk,
the posturing and pacing from the morning till the dusk.

I handed back the keys to the kingdom of the men,
and I wouldn't trade the loss to be a ghost in there again!

'Cause I was the spy in the boys' club, standing by the wall.
I saw the golden pedestal. I saw the way it falls.
I had the golden ticket, the unearned benefit,
but the armor was a cage, and the jacket didn't fit!
So I left my deep disguise hanging on the leather chair
and walked right out the fortress with the wind in my hair!

*(Bluesy, dissonant piano. A dramatic pause.)*

Yeah, it costs a little capital to take a different door.
You don't get the benefit of the doubt anymore.
But the freedom of the exit? Oh, it's worth the sudden drop
when you finally stop pretending you're the ruler at the top.

*(A snare roll into a brassy cabaret strut. Piano and guitar trade solos. The OFFICE MEN try to keep up.)*

## Notes for the composer

- A slinky cabaret strut: finger snaps, upright bass, smoky piano, then a brassy blowout. It's Act Two's one sunny number before things go dark, so let it be fun.
- Optional: if Act Two runs long, this is the first cut.

## What changed from the record

- Nearly nothing. A few small grammar fixes; "You don't get the 'benefit of the doubt' anymore" lost its quotation marks.
- New staging: the office man explaining her own diagram back to her during verse 2.
- "the jacket didn't fit" now lands on the show's coat image without any rewriting.

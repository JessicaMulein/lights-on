# Javert in a Cubicle

**Act Two · #27** · Sung by JENNIFER, THE ADJUSTER  
**Status:** Stage draft 1  
**Source:** "Javert in a Cubicle," and "Out of Stock" (the money)

**Plan:** The claim, and the man who might read it. The Adjuster as the ever-present fear (he never actually flags anything). Give him his own verse.

**THE ADJUSTER** *(reasonable, tired, sincere)*
I've got eleven hundred files and a mortgage of my own.
Somebody has to read the fine print. Somebody has to phone.
You'd be amazed at what people claim, and what a camera shows.

like five minutes on a good day makes a full-time working machine.

## Notes for the composer

- Dark outlaw-country ballad: minor key, galloping train-beat, twangy baritone guitar, pedal steel, lonesome harmonica.
- The Adjuster's verse should be in the same tune but more reasonable, almost gentle. He believes every word.

## What changed from the record

- "and he's hunted me across the years" became "and he's been dogging me for years." "Hunted you across the years" echoes Javert's line in the Les Misérables musical, which is still under copyright, so it's out. Javert, Valjean and the bishop come from Victor Hugo's novel, which is public domain, so the names stay.
- The Adjuster's verse is new.
- Verse 2 now says "What if he sees..." instead of "He saw...," because he never actually flagged anything. The fear is the story.
- The "seventy-two hundred a month" verse is new, built from Out of Stock without the GoFundMe. **That's your real number.** If you'd rather not put it on stage, swap in "Every month, one deposit."
- "Claim status: pending" is split: he says it, and she answers "Still pending."
